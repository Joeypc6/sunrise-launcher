A second user for this, with one extra requirement: a direction-aware swap.

**Storage Drawers' framed drawers** (a port in progress). A framed drawer item carries three materials in its data: side, trim and front. Each can be any full opaque block. The item is drawn with each part in its material's textures. Java does this with a custom item renderer (`DrawersItemRenderer`) that draws each face with the material block's icon *for that side*. A log's top differs from its side, and a furnace's front differs from its other faces.

**Fit with this issue.** Option 1 or 2 here covers it. The drawer's item model gives the three parts distinct placeholder sprites. A per-stack swap, "on this stack, the faces that use placeholder A draw with sprite B", then maps the three stored materials onto them.

**What would make it 1:1:** let the swap name the replacement per face direction. Either a map `(placeholder sprite, Direction) → sprite`, or a hook that sees each quad's direction. A single replacement sprite per placeholder still works, but drawers framed in logs or furnaces would then look wrong.

**The same thing for special model renderers.** `SpecialModelCollector::submit_model` submits geometry baked at resource load. A sprite remap option on that submission (`SpecialModelSubmitOptions` carrying `(sprite, Option<Direction>) → sprite`) would give special renderers the same per-stack retexture.

**Why pre-baking doesn't work.** On main 8207f6d6aa, `SpecialModelBakeContext` offers `bake_model` and `bake_geometry` but no read of other blocks' sprites, and the factory gets no host. Even with that read, pre-baking every (part, material block, direction) combination would mean hundreds of blocks in vanilla alone, times 3 parts.

Until this lands, the port shows the unframed item look. Placed framed drawers draw their materials through the block entity renderer.
